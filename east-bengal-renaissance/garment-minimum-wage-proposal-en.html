A Tk 30,000 Minimum Wage for Bangladesh’s Garment Workers

East Bengal Renaissance · 4 November 2026

East Bengal Renaissance calls on Bangladesh’s Minimum Wage Board to set a gross monthly minimum wage of Tk 30,000 for regular working hours in the ready-made garment industry, with overtime paid separately.

Why Tk 30,000?

The Anker Research Institute estimates that in 2025 a family of four in Dhaka needed Tk 48,022 a month for a basic but decent standard of living. Assuming income equivalent to 1.58 full-time workers per household, that implies a gross living wage of Tk 30,459 per worker. The corresponding benchmark for the industrial areas around Dhaka is Tk 25,166. A Tk 30,000 minimum is close to the Dhaka benchmark. Tk 18,000–20,000 falls below both benchmarks. Dhaka benchmark · Surrounding areas benchmark

How should the Board assess export competitiveness?

Tk 30,000 is about US$243, above Cambodia’s garment-sector minimum of US$210 and Vietnam’s highest regional minimum of about US$201. Buyers compare the total cost of receiving garments made to the same specifications, in the same quantity and on the same schedule. Fabric costs, productivity, duties and freight all affect that comparison. Cambodia’s minimum wage · Vietnam’s government figures · US International Trade Commission analysis

A US tariff example

In 2023, the average US import value per item for men’s and boys’ cotton trousers and shorts was US$6.12 from Bangladesh, US$7.62 from India and US$7.98 from Vietnam. Suppose 10% of Bangladesh’s starting price pays workers whose wages would rise from Tk 12,500 to Tk 30,000. If other costs and output stay constant and the buyer covers the full increase, the price rises to about US$6.98 per item. Import unit-value analysis

  • Bangladesh after the wage increase — US$8.83 per item with a 16.6% general duty and a 10% additional duty.
  • India — US$9.65 per item with a 16.6% general duty and a 10% additional duty.
  • Vietnam — US$10.30 per item with a 16.6% general duty and a 12.5% additional duty.

These figures exclude freight. If the affected wages account for 20% of Bangladesh’s starting price, its duty-inclusive cost rises to about US$9.92, above India’s figure in this example. The calculation uses historical category averages, an assumed share of labour costs and illustrative tariff rates. For an actual order, compare quotes for equivalent garments and verify the tariff code, rates and exclusions in effect on the date of entry. US Harmonized Tariff Schedule · US additional-duty notice

The European Union requires a separate calculation. Eligible Bangladeshi goods can enter duty-free under Everything But Arms (EBA). For Vietnam, the applicable product rate and rules of origin under the EU–Vietnam trade agreement matter. The EU has withdrawn EBA preferences for some Cambodian garments. Three requests to the Minimum Wage Board

  1. Set the monthly minimum wage at Tk 30,000. Publish pay rates by grade and explain how overtime pay is calculated.
  2. Publish the export competitiveness analysis. Obtain comparable quotes for T-shirts, trousers and sweaters from India, Vietnam and Cambodia. Compare them with Bangladesh’s prices after adding freight and duties for the US and EU markets.
  3. Seek written commitments from international brands. Publish how much of the additional labour cost each participating brand commits to covering through higher purchase prices.

Living-cost data support the case for Tk 30,000. If buyers are expected to move orders elsewhere, the Board should publish which products and markets are at risk and how large the duty-inclusive cost gap would be.
