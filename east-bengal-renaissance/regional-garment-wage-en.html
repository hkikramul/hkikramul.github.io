Garment Workers’ Minimum Wage: A Divisional Proposal Based on Anker Benchmarks

East Bengal Renaissance · 23 November 2026

Instead of demanding a single national wage of Tk 30,000 for garment workers, could wages be set according to the cost of living where they work? Published benchmarks from the Anker Research Institute and the Global Living Wage Coalition provide a strong starting point. Bangladesh does not yet have equally detailed Anker research for every district, however. It is essential to distinguish directly measured benchmarks from our own divisional estimates.

Our revised proposal

Set one equal monthly gross minimum wage for women and men in each industrial labour market, for standard working hours. Where Anker has published a direct benchmark, use it as the initial reference point. The figures below for other divisions are provisional estimates; they should be finalized only after validation and revision through local field research using the Anker methodology. Overtime pay remains additional to the minimum wage.

Other divisional figures are also not Anker-published benchmarks; they are preliminary estimates made with the localization formula described below.

How were the divisional figures calculated?

Anker’s latest publicly displayed numeric benchmarks are Tk 30,459 per month for Dhaka City and Tk 25,166 for satellite cities and districts surrounding Dhaka. Both assume a four-person family with 1.58 earners. Anker’s Bangladesh page also lists 2026 update reports, but the revised 2026 amounts are not displayed on the report landing page. We therefore use the verifiable 2025 figures as the direct anchors in this proposal. Dhaka City benchmark · Satellite cities benchmark · Anker’s Bangladesh benchmarks and update reports

Anker has not published direct living-wage amounts for the rural and urban areas of all eight divisions. For locations beyond Dhaka, we use the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics’ (BBS) 2022 Upper Poverty Line (UPL) as a limited proxy for relative regional costs. The formula is:

Proposed divisional wage = Tk 30,459 × (local UPL ÷ Dhaka City UPL of Tk 4,922)

The results are rounded to the nearest Tk 100. The UPL ratio captures some geographic variation in the cost of basic needs, but it is not a full living-cost estimate, a current price index or an Anker benchmark. The formula assumes every part of the living-cost basket rises or falls in the same proportion, which may not hold in practice. Rent, transport, healthcare, childcare, working hours and the number of earners vary by location.

Why are these figures not direct Anker benchmarks?

The Anker Methodology collects local food prices, costs of decent housing, healthcare, education, transport and other essential needs. It involves workers and local stakeholders, and specifies family size and the number of earners. A wage estimate based only on poverty-line ratios is not equivalent to the Anker Methodology. Anker conducted a rural study in Jhenidah and Alladargah in 2025 and lists a 2026 update. Once the full local benchmark figures are directly verified, they should replace our proxy for those areas. How could this be applied at district level?

The available figures do not support calling 64 different amounts Anker benchmarks. Anker’s direct Dhaka-area benchmark applies to selected industrial locations, while its new rural benchmark covers specific areas in Jhenidah and Alladargah. The divisional figures for other locations are only provisional starting points. BBS, worker organizations and employers should conduct representative surveys of local prices, rent, transport, healthcare and education costs in factory-dense districts. Each local industrial labour market should then receive its own estimate using the same Anker methodology.

Administrative district boundaries do not always match workers’ commuting and residential areas. The centre of Dhaka, factory areas in Gazipur and workers’ housing in Narayanganj may have different costs. Wage zones should therefore follow actual factory clusters and workers’ labour markets, not district names alone.

Principles for the proposal

  1. Set one minimum wage for women and men in the same location.
  2. Do not count overtime, uncertain bonuses or uncertain benefits as part of the Anker living wage.
  3. Update rates annually for inflation and fully re-estimate local benchmarks with the Anker Methodology every few years.
  4. Obtain written purchasing-price commitments from brands and buyers so wage increases are not shifted entirely onto supplier factories and workers.

In short, the direct Anker figures currently available are Tk 30,459 for Dhaka City and Tk 25,166 for satellite industrial areas. The figures proposed for other divisions are provisional estimates made with a transparent formula, not final benchmarks. The next step toward a fair district-level wage is to replace those estimates with current local price data.

Calculate a wage for a decent life in Bangladesh

Enter your household’s essential monthly costs to estimate a wage target for your circumstances. The calculator subtracts other reliable household income, then divides the remaining need among full-time-equivalent earners and adjusts for deductions. It is a personal budgeting aid, not an official rate or a substitute for full Anker field research. No data is sent to or stored on a server.

Monthly household budget (Tk)

Enter your costs and select “Calculate wage.”

Formula: (essential household costs × (1 + contingency rate) − other reliable household income) ÷ (1 − deduction rate) ÷ full-time-equivalent earners = required monthly gross wage per earner. Hourly equivalent = monthly gross wage × 12 ÷ (weekly hours × 52). A negative result is treated as zero.

The economics: from living costs to a wage target

To make “how much is needed?” measurable, wage-setting must account for household essentials, the number of earners, local prices and deductions. Anker’s direct 2025 benchmarks assume a family of four and 1.58 earners. A nationally researched benchmark and the household calculator above are different: the former uses local field research; the latter uses a household’s own budget.

Household net-income target = essential monthly costs × (1 + contingency rate) − other reliable household income.
Gross wage per earner = household net-income target ÷ (1 − deduction/tax rate) ÷ number of full-time-equivalent earners.

Example: Dhaka’s 2025 Anker benchmark is Tk 30,459 and the satellite-industrial-area benchmark is Tk 25,166 per month. Comparing them with the previously established RMG statutory floor of Tk 12,500 gives a Dhaka gap of Tk 17,959: the floor covers 41.0% of the benchmark and would need to rise by about 143.7% to reach it. The satellite-area gap is Tk 12,666: the floor covers 49.7%, implying an increase of about 101.3% to reach the benchmark. These are illustrations anchored to the earlier legal floor; they must be recalculated if the wage board issues a new gazette.

To assess the effect of wage increases on factory competitiveness, we need a unit labour-cost calculation. A simple formula is: incremental direct wage cost per garment = (new monthly wage − old monthly wage) ÷ saleable garments produced per worker per month. At an illustrative output of 500 saleable garments per worker per month, the additional Tk 17,959 to reach the Dhaka benchmark is about Tk 35.92 per garment; the additional Tk 12,666 for the satellite benchmark is Tk 25.33. At 400/600 garments, the figures are Dhaka Tk 44.90/Tk 29.93 and satellite Tk 31.67/Tk 21.11.

This is a sensitivity illustration, not an actual factory cost sheet. We would need garment type, SAM (standard allowed minutes), line efficiency, product complexity, rejects, indirect labour, employer social contributions, FOB price and buyer negotiations to estimate export competitiveness. A fuller formula is: wage-cost share of FOB (%) = wage cost per garment ÷ garment FOB price × 100. Buyer price adjustments, productivity and responsible purchasing practices therefore belong in the analysis.

How wage-setting works in advanced social-democratic systems

Denmark has no single statutory national minimum wage. Most pay is negotiated in collective agreements between trade unions and employer organizations, by occupation or sector and with provisions for skills, seniority, working hours and supplements. There is therefore no single Danish legal wage number comparable to an RMG statutory minimum; the relevant garment-sector collective agreement must be examined. The system depends on strong collective bargaining and agreement coverage, so the rate cannot simply be copied.

For a numerical example from a high-income European economy, Germany’s general statutory floor is €13.90/hour in 2026. At a 40-hour week, the monthly equivalent is €13.90 × 40 × 52 ÷ 12 = about €2,409 gross. From 1 January 2027, the €14.60 rate gives about €2,531/month using the same formula. The government implements the Minimum Wage Commission’s recommendation by regulation. This is not an RMG-specific rate, and nominal conversion alone is inadequate: productivity, prices, taxes, social contributions and exchange rates differ.

ILO principles: no single global wage figure

The ILO does not set one universal minimum-wage number for every country. Under Article 3 of the Minimum Wage Fixing Convention, 1970 (No. 131), national authorities consider workers’ and families’ needs—including the cost of living, general wage levels, social security benefits and relative living standards—alongside economic factors such as development requirements, productivity and maintaining high employment. Article 4 calls for an effective wage-fixing system, full consultation with representative worker and employer organizations, and their participation where appropriate. Regular adjustment and enforcement also matter.

Economically, this is a two-stage decision: (1) estimate a needs-based target from reliable local costs and household labour participation; (2) assess and negotiate that target using wage distribution, inflation, productivity, employment and industry-capacity evidence, together with social dialogue. The ILO does not mandate one formula. The household calculator makes the needs side visible; it does not replace the national institutions and social partners that must set the actual minimum wage.
