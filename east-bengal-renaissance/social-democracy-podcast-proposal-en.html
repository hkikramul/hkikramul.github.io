A Social Democratic Podcast Proposal for Bangladesh

A proposal for a bilingual podcast on labour, public health, agriculture, democracy, civic rights and foreign policy. The programme groups 59 topic areas into six themes and sets out ten episode formats.

Each episode asks who makes the decisions, who bears the costs and risks, who benefits, and how much power ordinary people have to change the outcome.

Episode formats

Explainer
Take one idea, one familiar example and one calculation. How are minimum wages set?
Field reporting
Follow a product from the early-morning wholesale market to a neighbourhood shop.
First-person account
Hear from a worker who took on recruitment debt to migrate for work.
Fact-check
Test claims about garment workers’ wage purchasing power across countries.
Following the money
Break down where the price of a T-shirt goes.
Oral history
Record several firsthand accounts of the July uprising outside Dhaka.
Policy debate
Compare costs, benefits and implementation for universal and income-targeted pensions.
Accountability interview
Compare referral responsibilities with patients’ experiences of reaching district hospitals.
Comparative episode
Compare garment wage-setting in Bangladesh and Vietnam.
Listener Q&A
How can a resident submit a proposal for a ward budget?

Editorial approach

An episode can open with the experience of a worker, farmer, service user or local resident. A researcher can use evidence to assess how widely that experience is shared. A decision-maker can explain what they control and how outcomes would be measured. Episodes will distinguish verified facts, personal accounts, allegations and political views. People or institutions facing specific allegations will have a chance to respond. For sensitive interviews, agree on what identifying information may be published before recording.

Three possible opening episodes are whether households can get by without overtime, how illness affects the family budget, and who sets the local budget.
