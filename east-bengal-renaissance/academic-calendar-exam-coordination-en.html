Calendars Are a Measure of State Capacity: Coordinating University and Recruitment Exams

When does a semester begin? When will classes end? When are final exams and public recruitment tests? Timely answers are not a luxury. They show basic respect for students’ and job seekers’ time, money and opportunities.

বাংলা সংস্করণ পড়ুন

Watching university administration in Bangladesh can leave students wondering whether a large institution can maintain a dependable calendar at all. At the university where I study, students, faculty and staff know before a semester begins when classes will start and end, and when final examinations are scheduled. In many places in Bangladesh, students still piece together the semester from separate notices issued by departments, batches or individual teachers.

Precision matters: it would be inaccurate to say that no Bangladeshi university has an academic calendar. The University of Dhaka provides department- and batch-level calendars on its website, with information such as class start, in-course assessment, class completion and final-exam start dates. Some other universities also publish calendars. The problem is that access, currency and consistency vary. At some institutions information is missing or hard to find; elsewhere it may be unclear how to learn about revisions. A regular, advance and updated calendar should be the norm, not an exception.

Who pays when there is no reliable calendar?

An academic calendar is more than an administrative document. Students need it to plan jobs, housing, travel, health care, family responsibilities and the next semester. Faculty can plan courses and assessments; staff can coordinate rooms, examination halls, papers and results. When dates shift without adequate notice or exams are postponed indefinitely, students bear the cost—especially those who travel from distant districts, work while studying or have limited resources.

Uniformity across every department is not the answer. Laboratories, clinical placements, fieldwork, internships and research have different needs. But flexibility does not have to mean uncertainty. Each program can have its own plan while publishing dates, the responsible office, reasons for changes and the revised schedule.

Recruitment exams: one day can mean losing an opportunity

Weak coordination in recruitment exams directly reduces job seekers’ options. A 2021 Prothom Alo report described 19 organisations holding recruitment exams on one day, across morning, noon and afternoon sessions; some tests overlapped, preventing applicants from sitting for more than one. A separate report covered 14 employment tests scheduled on the same day. These stories are examples, not a census of every institution or exam, but they document a recurring problem.

I also remember clashes among university admission-test schedules, including SUST and RUET, around the period when I was applying. Applicants then had to choose between institutions, travel and exam times. Capacity, question security and institutional autonomy mean that every university test cannot be merged into one. But authorities can identify date conflicts in advance, consider distant test centres and avoid unnecessary simultaneous exams.

Regional and international examples: make calendars routine

India: In a 2024 letter, the University Grants Commission reminded higher-education institutions to follow academic calendars. The letter described calendars as including the start of classes, examinations, vacations and the end of semesters, and explained that advance publication gives students and other stakeholders visibility into planned activities. For recruitment, India’s Union Public Service Commission says it publishes the annual calendar for structured examinations in the next calendar year at least six months in advance. The practical lesson for Bangladesh is that a public schedule is an administrative responsibility that can be assigned and timed.

South Korea: Universities including Korea University, KAIST and Seoul National University make semester calendars publicly available. Registration, classes, examinations and holidays appear in an online schedule. Exact dates vary by institution; the shared feature is that information is accessible outside internal offices.

UNLV in the United States: The university publishes semester dates and a separate final-examination schedule. It also tells students what to do if they have three or more finals on one day, or two at the same time: contact the relevant department about alternatives. Publishing dates matters, and so does explaining how to seek a remedy when conflicts occur.

These examples do not show that every university in these countries is well run, or prove the single cause of Bangladesh’s administrative problems. They show that advance information and conflict-resolution rules can be built into routine administration.

Politicisation and administrative competence

My political assessment is that party loyalty, patronage and political allocation of posts play a major role in weakening university administration. But one missing calendar cannot prove that every teacher is incompetent or that politics alone explains every university problem. Calendar delays, postponed exams and unaccountable decisions should be treated as measurable administrative outcomes, then examined alongside appointment, promotion and accountability systems.

The legal distinction matters too. Rule 25 of Bangladesh’s Government Servants (Conduct) Rules, 1979 restricts political-party membership and political activity for government servants. The terms of service for university teachers and employees may instead be governed by their own laws, orders and statutes. For example, section 46(2) of the Dhaka University Order, 1973 protects teachers’ freedom to hold political views and associate with lawful organisations outside the university. So it is too broad to say simply that government employees cannot be political but university teachers can. Holding political views, civic association, holding a party office, electoral politics and using partisan influence over a university administrative appointment are distinct questions.

My objection is not to a teacher’s freedom to hold political views. It is to administrative responsibility being distributed through party loyalty rather than published qualifications, with no accountability when the work fails. Appointment criteria, terms, responsibilities and performance reviews for administrative posts should be public. No one should be punished for a political opinion; people should face due process when they fail to carry out their responsibilities.

Practical demands for a youth-led reform movement

  1. Publish every semester calendar in advance: Universities and departments should publish it at least 8–12 weeks before classes begin. Include class dates, in-course assessments, teaching completion, final-exam windows, target result dates, holidays and a responsible contact office.
  2. One searchable directory with revision history: The UGC should maintain a university-by-university directory of current program calendars. Show version dates, previous versions, reasons for changes and revised deadlines.
  3. A university admission coordination desk: The UGC and admissions authorities should submit announced dates to a shared online calendar. Flag likely conflicts before schedules are final; explain the reason for same-day exams and the arrangements for distant centres.
  4. A national public recruitment-exam calendar: The Public Service Commission, Ministry of Public Administration, recruiting agencies and Bankers’ Selection Committee could maintain an open register of notices and exam dates. Publish tentative dates before applications open, avoid simultaneous tests where possible and issue prompt notices when a date changes.
  5. A remedy for exam conflicts: If a written, oral or practical test overlaps with another recruitment exam, applicants need a clear window to report it, a named official responsible for review and a deadline for a decision. Not every exam can be moved, but losing an opportunity without even checking a conflict is unacceptable.
  6. An annual public performance report: Universities and recruiting bodies should publish how many exams took place on schedule, how many were postponed and why, and the average delay in publishing results. Good performance becomes visible, and administrative failure becomes harder to conceal.

Closing point

This is not mainly a technology problem. It is a problem of priorities, responsibility and coordination. A calendar page alone will not make an administration competent. But without one, students do not know whom to ask, job seekers cannot see why an opportunity disappeared, and administrative failure cannot be measured.

If young people want to build a new kind of politics, these demands belong on the agenda: advance academic calendars, one place to find university and recruitment exam dates, a remedy when dates clash, and public accountability when institutions miss their commitments. The grand language of reform can come later. First, tell people on time when the exam will be held.
