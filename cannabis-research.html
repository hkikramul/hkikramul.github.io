Bangladesh and alternative datasets

  • WHO Bangladesh GSHS 2014: public microdata catalog — Q41 lifetime and Q42 current marijuana use, plus mental-health and protective factors. Older school-based data, not all youth or adults.
  • Q42 catalog — 21 unweighted nonzero current-use reports; prioritize descriptive uncertainty rather than an elaborate local regression.
  • Bangladesh student substance-use repository — candidate supplemental data; cannabis-specific measurement, license and sample design still need verification.
  • CDC national YRBS — alternative for school-age cannabis and mental-health indicators; not a CUD diagnosis and not directly interchangeable with NSDUH.

03 / ANNOTATED READING

What is known—and what this study adds.

Working synthesis: Existing evidence already examines cannabis, depressive symptoms, disorder and suicidality. Longitudinal and cross-sectional designs answer different questions. A 2024 analysis may update descriptive evidence, but a new survey year alone does not establish a novel contribution. The proposed addition is contemporary disorder-focused estimation with transparent absolute/relative uncertainty and a carefully limited Bangladesh discussion.

This is a targeted annotated reading list, not a completed systematic review. Read the full texts, compare populations, outcomes and adjustment sets, and document overlap before finalizing the research question. Sources [6–7] were identified through bibliographic abstracts; full-text appraisal remains pending.

READING 4

Gobbi et al. (2019): adolescent cannabis and later depression, anxiety and suicidality

Systematic review and meta-analysis. Useful background on timing; its adolescent longitudinal evidence differs from this adult cross-sectional design. Observational confounding remains relevant.

Literature-review extraction checklist

For each paper record: design; country and ages; survey years; exposure definition; DSM version and outcome; recall period; sample and exclusions; weights/design; adjustment set; estimate and confidence interval; temporal ordering; missingness; selection bias; funding; and relevance to Bangladesh. Avoid pooling unlike outcomes as “addiction.”

Suggested PubMed query: (cannabis OR marijuana) AND ("cannabis use disorder" OR depression OR "major depressive episode") AND ("young adults" OR NSDUH). Run a separate Bangladesh search and an independent policy-evaluation search. Record search date, inclusion choices and a concise reason for each exclusion.

04 / ANALYSIS WORKFLOW

One focused biomedical question.

Primary question: Among observed U.S. young-adult past-year cannabis users, how does CUD prevalence differ between those with and without past-year MDE? Report associations; do not infer which condition caused the other.

  1. Freeze the protocol. Review novelty, clinical rationale and institutional research determination with an adviser. Define primary estimands and exclusions before inspecting model associations.
  2. Audit the file. Record version/checksum, labels and nonresponse/skip rules; count the domain and outcome groups. Verify official derived variables.
  3. Declare the design on the full file. Use the documented weight, stratum and nested PSU, then estimate the age/use domain. Do not fit ordinary independent-record regression.
  4. Describe burden. Show unweighted N, weighted prevalence, 95% intervals, missingness and CUD-by-MDE cells. Assess design precision and sparse covariate categories.
  5. Fit the prespecified core model. Survey-weighted logistic regression with age group, recorded sex, race/ethnicity, education, poverty and insurance. Its coefficient is an odds ratio. The original protocol additionally calls for design-aware standardized prevalence differences and ratios.
  6. Review sensitivities. Cigarette/alcohol adjustment, residual missingness, influential weights and optional frequency adjustment. Frequency conditioning changes the question and may overadjust. Poverty exclusions involving dorm residents need particular attention.
  7. Report uncertainty and limits. No stepwise selection; no post-hoc causal narrative. Address selection into cannabis use, bidirectionality, self-report, screening and limited transportability.
Analysis code status: The downloadable R starter implements data checks, survey descriptives and logistic regression, with an optional modified-Poisson prevalence-ratio model. That optional ratio differs from the original standardized marginal ratio. Standardized margins and extended sensitivity models still require implementation and adviser approval. R is unavailable in the authoring environment, so this script has not been executed or validated on the data. No generated model results are presented.

Do not substitute ordinary bootstrap resampling of records for the survey design. Preserve official imputed fields and distinguish structural missingness from nonresponse. The starter stops for review when core complete-case exclusions reach 5%; this operational threshold does not establish that smaller exclusions are harmless. If imputation is needed, incorporate the design and analysis variables with appropriate pooling.

Bangladesh policy interpretation

Use this evidence to identify screening, treatment and surveillance questions. Do not transport a U.S. prevalence estimate to Bangladesh or conclude that changing a ban improves health. Medical access, decriminalization and commercial legalization are distinct interventions. Medical benefits require indication-specific clinical evidence; policy effects require a credible policy design and geographically identified data.
