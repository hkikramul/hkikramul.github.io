Reading questions and thesis relevance

Why multiply a rate by a group size? A rate per person multiplied by the number of people gives a total flow. Infection transfers people from S to I; recovery transfers them from I to R.

What does standard incidence mean? The infection flow is βSI/N. At the same infectious fraction, changing population size leaves the per-susceptible infection hazard unchanged.

How does this support the thesis? Understanding rates and units supports simulation validation. Distinguishing prevalence from incidence determines whether an area measures infectious person-time or cumulative infections. Repeated stochastic runs provide a sample of finite-horizon areas for distribution fitting.

What requires adviser confirmation? The actual working model, the curve being fitted, observation horizon, initial conditions, parameter scenarios, and whether stochastic births/deaths preserve population size exactly. These notes do not establish distribution-fitting novelty.

Reading-note convention: Future requested updates in this conversation should explain intuition, symbols and units, derivation, a worked example, statistical meaning, assumptions, and relevance to the agreed thesis scope. Newly identified relevant material should be added with source attribution and version tracking; proposed extensions remain subject to adviser confirmation.
