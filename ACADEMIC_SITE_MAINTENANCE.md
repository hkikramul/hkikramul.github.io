# Academic website maintenance

Public pages: index.html, research.html, teaching.html, presentations.html, notes.html. Edit these files directly; these pages no longer load the legacy content-loader.js, so stale CMS JSON cannot overwrite the revised layout. The legacy editor continues to serve other existing content; its old academic fields do not control these pages.

Keep homepage updates limited to meaningful research/teaching milestones. Update accepted versus delivered status after events. Keep working schedules, tentative conference targets and unpublished protocols in private planning files; Google Calendar holds scheduled commitments. Review links and dates each semester. Keep Making Sense independent.

Preserve manuscript version history on research.html. Do not infer completion from scheduled work.

## Audience and content ownership
Academic site: biography, CV, research projects/manuscripts, delivered/accepted research presentations, teaching appointments/philosophy/course handouts, seminar records and project-specific scholarly references.
Making Sense: introductory lessons and interactive tools, graduate tutorial notes, conceptual method explainers, general biomedical learning pathway, educator reading guide, public essays, books/history and films. Maintain full learning resources only there and link from academic pages.
Making Sense static page layout/text is edited in HTML; lessons and searchable resources still load from content/learning/lessons.json and resources.json via app.js. Legacy CMS text fields do not control revised static pages.

## Resource discovery
Use the same six primary links plus Focus & Goals on all academic pages. Presentations remain linked under Research. notes.html is the searchable Study Resources entry point; Making Sense keeps the full graduate tutorial content, linked directly from the index. When adding a resource, add a matching resource-item to the index with a descriptive title, topic, data-search keywords and direct file/reference links. Keep individual expert links accessible. Preserve existing URLs and anchors. Do not label dated seminars upcoming after their date without checking their status; a seminar record does not imply attendance.
