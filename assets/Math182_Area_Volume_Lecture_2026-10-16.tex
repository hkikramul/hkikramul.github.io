\topic{1. One slice explains the formulas}
Start with a bounded region. Draw one thin rectangle and identify its length and thickness. For area, add the rectangles. For volume, rotate them and add the resulting pieces.
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[>=Stealth]
\draw[fill=softblue] (0,0) rectangle (2.4,1.5);
\draw[<->] (0,-.25)--(2.4,-.25)node[midway,below]{thickness};
\draw[<->](-.25,0)--(-.25,1.5)node[midway,left]{length};
\node[align=left,anchor=west]at(3.1,.8){Small area = length $\times$ thickness\\Thin rectangles become exact in the limit.};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
The rectangle above is schematic: an actual integration slice is very thin.
\[
A=\int_a^b[\text{top}-\text{bottom}]\,dx,
\qquad
A=\int_c^d[\text{right}-\text{left}]\,dy.
\]
\topic{Choose the slice, then choose the formula}
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{p{1.2in}p{2.0in}p{2.8in}}\toprule
Method & Slice relative to axis & Small volume\\\midrule
Disk & Perpendicular; reaches axis & $\pi R^2\,dt$\\[5pt]
Washer & Perpendicular; has a hole & $\pi(R^2-r^2)\,dt$\\[5pt]
Shell & Parallel & $2\pi rh\,dt$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
Here $dt$ is $dx$ or $dy$. Radii are distances to the rotation axis. The shell height $h$ is the length of the slice, even when that length is horizontal.
\textbf{Setup routine:} sketch and shade $\to$ find bounds $\to$ draw a slice $\to$ label distances $\to$ write the integral $\to$ evaluate and check.

\topic{2. Example 1. Area between a line and a parabola}
Find the area enclosed by $y=x+2$ and $y=x^2$.

\textbf{Intersections:} $x^2=x+2$ gives $(x-2)(x+1)=0$, so $x=-1,2$. The line lies above the parabola on this interval.
\begin{center}\begin{tikzpicture}[x=0.8cm,y=0.55cm,>=Stealth]
\fill[gray!20] plot[domain=-1:2,samples=60](\x,{\x+2}) -- plot[domain=2:-1,samples=60](\x,{\x*\x}) -- cycle;
\draw[->](-1.6,0)--(2.8,0)node[right]{$x$};\draw[->](0,-.3)--(0,4.8)node[above]{$y$};
\draw[thick,domain=-1.3:2.2]plot(\x,{\x+2});\draw[thick,domain=-1.4:2.15,samples=70]plot(\x,{\x*\x});
\draw[very thick](.7,.49)--(.7,2.7);\node[right]at(.7,1.5){vertical slice};
\node[left]at(-1,1){$(-1,1)$};\node[right]at(2,4){$(2,4)$};
\end{tikzpicture}\end{center}
\step{1}{Find the intersections}
The equation $x^2=x+2$ identifies the endpoints of the enclosed region. Its solutions are $-1$ and $2$, so these become the $x$-limits.
\step{2}{Identify top and bottom}
At $x=0$, the line has height $2$ and the parabola has height $0$. Furthermore, $(x+2)-x^2=(2-x)(x+1)\geq0$ throughout $[-1,2]$.
\step{3}{Measure the slice}
The slice has height $(x+2)-x^2$ and thickness $dx$. Therefore
\[
A=\int_{-1}^2(x+2-x^2)\,dx
=\left[\frac{x^2}{2}+2x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^2
=\frac{10}{3}-\left(-\frac76\right)
=\boxed{\frac92\text{ square units}}.
\]
\textbf{Check your understanding.} Why must we subtract the parabola from the line? Because a slice length is top minus bottom and must be nonnegative.
\intuition{The integral adds the vertical gaps between the curves. The bottom curve need not be the $x$-axis. Reversing the subtraction gives a negative signed integral, not the geometric area.}
\textbf{Prompt:} ``Point to the top and bottom of one slice before writing any formula.''

\topic{3. Example 2: a slice becomes a disk}
\textbf{Problem.} Rotate the region under $y=\sqrt{4-x}$, above the $x$-axis, for $0\leq x\leq4$, about the $x$-axis.
\begin{center}\begin{tikzpicture}[>=Stealth]
\begin{scope}[x=.85cm,y=.85cm]
\fill[softblue](0,0)--plot[domain=0:4,samples=60](\x,{sqrt(4-\x)})--cycle;
\draw[->](0,0)--(4.6,0)node[right]{$x$};\draw[->](0,0)--(0,2.5)node[above]{$y$};
\draw[thick,domain=0:4,samples=60]plot(\x,{sqrt(4-\x)});
\draw[inkblue,line width=3pt](1,0)--(1,{sqrt(3)});
\node[above right]at(1,{sqrt(3)}){$R=\sqrt{4-x}$};
\node[below]at(4,0){$4$};\node[below]at(1,0){$x$};
\end{scope}
\draw[->,thick](5,1)--(6,1);
\begin{scope}[shift={(8,1)}]
\filldraw[fill=softblue] (0,0) circle (1.05);
\draw[->,inkblue,thick](0,0)--(.74,.74)node[midway,below right]{$R$};
\fill(0,0)circle(1.5pt);\node[below]at(0,-1.3){Cross-section: $\pi R^2$};
\end{scope}
\end{tikzpicture}\end{center}
\step{1}{Understand the region}
The curve passes through $(0,2)$ and $(4,0)$. The region lies between the curve and $y=0$.
\step{2}{Choose the slice}
The rotation axis is horizontal. A vertical slice is perpendicular to it, has thickness $dx$, and extends from the axis to the curve.
\step{3}{Measure the radius}
The distance from $y=0$ to $y=\sqrt{4-x}$ is $R(x)=\sqrt{4-x}$. There is no hole: $r=0$.
\step{4}{Write the small volume and integral}
\[
dV=\pi\left(\sqrt{4-x}\right)^2dx=\pi(4-x)\,dx,
\qquad V=\pi\int_0^4(4-x)\,dx.
\]
\step{5}{Evaluate}
\[
V=\pi\left[4x-\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^4
=\pi[(16-8)-0]=\boxed{8\pi\text{ cubic units}}.
\]
\intuition{Imagine stacking thin coins with changing radii. The circular area is $\pi R^2$; multiplying by the small thickness produces volume. The square root disappears because the radius is squared.}
\pauseq{Why does this problem produce a disk rather than a washer?}
\textit{Answer: the representative slice reaches the axis; there is no central gap.}

\topic{4. Example 3: a slice becomes a washer}
\textbf{Problem.} Rotate the region between $y=x+1$ and $y=1$, for $0\leq x\leq2$, about the $x$-axis.
\begin{center}\begin{tikzpicture}[>=Stealth]
\begin{scope}[x=1cm,y=.7cm]
\fill[softblue](0,1)--(2,3)--(2,1)--cycle;
\draw[->](0,0)--(3,0)node[right]{$x$};\draw[->](0,0)--(0,3.7)node[above]{$y$};
\draw[thick](0,1)--(2,3)node[above]{$y=x+1$};\draw[thick](0,1)--(2,1)node[right]{$y=1$};
\draw[inkblue,line width=3pt](1,1)--(1,2);
\draw[<->](2.6,0)--(2.6,2)node[midway,right]{$R$};
\draw[<->](.5,0)--(.5,1)node[midway,left]{$r$};
\end{scope}
\draw[->,thick](4.2,1)--(5.2,1);
\begin{scope}[shift={(7.5,1.2)}]
\fill[softblue,even odd rule](0,0)circle(1.25)(0,0)circle(.55);
\draw(0,0)circle(1.25);\draw(0,0)circle(.55);
\draw[->](0,0)--(-.9,.87)node[midway,left]{$R$};\draw[->](0,0)--(.55,0)node[midway,below]{$r$};
\node[below]at(0,-1.5){Area: $\pi R^2-\pi r^2$};
\end{scope}
\end{tikzpicture}\end{center}
\step{1}{Choose a vertical slice}
It is perpendicular to the horizontal rotation axis. It starts at $y=1$ and ends at $y=x+1$, so rotating it leaves a hole.
\step{2}{Measure both radii from the axis}
\[R=(x+1)-0=x+1,\qquad r=1-0=1.\]
The slice length is $x$, but the outer radius is $x+1$. A radius starts at the axis, not at the bottom curve.
\step{3}{Subtract disk areas and integrate}
\begin{align*}
V&=\pi\int_0^2[(x+1)^2-1^2]\,dx\\
 &=\pi\int_0^2(x^2+2x+1-1)\,dx
 =\pi\int_0^2(x^2+2x)\,dx\\
 &=\pi\left[\frac{x^3}{3}+x^2\right]_0^2
 =\pi\left(\frac83+4\right)=\boxed{\frac{20\pi}{3}}.
\end{align*}
\intuition{A washer is an outer disk with an inner disk removed. Subtract areas, not radii: $R^2-r^2\neq(R-r)^2$.}
\textbf{Shifted-axis extension (review).} About $y=-2$, the same region has
\[R=(x+1)-(-2)=x+3,\qquad r=1-(-2)=3.\]
\[
V=\pi\int_0^2[(x+3)^2-9]\,dx
=\pi\left[\frac{x^3}{3}+3x^2\right]_0^2
=\boxed{\frac{44\pi}{3}}.
\]

\topic{5. Cylindrical shells: parallel slices}
A slice parallel to the rotation axis sweeps out a thin cylindrical shell. Unrolling it gives a rectangle with length equal to its circumference.
\[
\boxed{dV=2\pi(\text{radius})(\text{height})(\text{thickness})}.
\]
The shell height is the length of the original slice. For a horizontal slice, this is a horizontal length.
\topic{Example 4. Horizontal shells about the $x$-axis}
Rotate the first-quadrant region bounded by $x=y^2$, $x=4$, and $y=0$ about the $x$-axis. Use shells.

\textbf{Bounds:} $y^2=4$ gives the upper height $y=2$. Thus $0\leq y\leq2$.

\textbf{One horizontal slice:}
\[
\text{radius}=y,\qquad
\text{height}=\text{right}-\text{left}=4-y^2,\qquad
\text{thickness}=dy.
\]
\begin{center}\begin{tikzpicture}[x=.8cm,y=.8cm,>=Stealth]
\fill[gray!20] (0,0)--(4,0)--(4,2)--plot[domain=2:0,samples=60]({\x*\x},\x)--cycle;
\draw[->](0,0)--(5,0)node[right]{$x$ (axis of rotation)};
\draw[->](0,0)--(0,2.7)node[above]{$y$};
\draw[thick,domain=0:2,samples=60]plot({\x*\x},\x);
\draw[thick](4,0)--(4,2);\draw[very thick](1,1)--(4,1);
\draw[<->](4.5,0)--(4.5,1)node[midway,right]{radius $y$};
\node[above]at(2.5,1){height $4-y^2$};
\node[right]at(4,2){$(4,2)$};\node[left]at(.49,.7){$x=y^2$};
\end{tikzpicture}\end{center}
\textbf{Why the bounds are $y$-values.} The slice moves upward from $y=0$ to $y=2$. For a horizontal slice, the thickness is $dy$; therefore its bounds must be heights.

\textbf{Evaluate step by step:}
\begin{align*}
V&=2\pi\int_0^2y(4-y^2)\,dy\\
 &=2\pi\left[2y^2-\frac{y^4}{4}\right]_0^2
=2\pi\left[(8-4)-(0-0)\right]=\boxed{8\pi\text{ cubic units}}.
\end{align*}
\textbf{Independent check:} vertical disks have radius $\sqrt{x}$ for $0\leq x\leq4$, so $V=\pi\int_0^4x\,dx=8\pi$.
\topic{Choose the direction before writing a formula}
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
Axis & Vertical slices ($dx$) & Horizontal slices ($dy$)\\\midrule
Horizontal & Disks/washers & Shells\\
Vertical & Shells & Disks/washers\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
\textbf{Vertical-shell model:} about the $y$-axis, a region with top $f(x)$ and bottom $g(x)$, contained in $x\geq0$, has
\[V=2\pi\int_a^b x[f(x)-g(x)]\,dx.\]
\intuition{Moving upward makes the shell radius larger but its horizontal length shorter. The integrand accounts for both changes. Do not square the shell height.}

\topic{6. Why the shell formula works}
\begin{center}\begin{tikzpicture}[>=Stealth]
\begin{scope}
\fill[softblue](0,1)--(3,1)arc(90:-90:.35 and 1)--(0,-1)arc(-90:90:.35 and 1);
\draw(0,0)ellipse(.35 and 1);\draw(3,0)ellipse(.35 and 1);\draw(0,1)--(3,1);\draw(0,-1)--(3,-1);
\draw[dashed,->](-.5,0)--(3.7,0)node[right]{axis};
\draw[<->](1.5,0)--(1.5,1)node[midway,right]{$r$};
\draw[<->](0,-1.35)--(3,-1.35)node[midway,below]{$h$};
\node[above]at(1.5,1.25){Cylindrical surface};
\end{scope}
\draw[->,thick](4.5,0)--(5.5,0);
\begin{scope}[shift={(6.2,-1)}]
\filldraw[fill=softblue](0,0)rectangle(3,2);
\node[above]at(1.5,2){Unrolled surface};
\draw[<->](0,-.3)--(3,-.3)node[midway,below]{$h$};
\draw[<->](3.3,0)--(3.3,2)node[midway,right]{$2\pi r$};
\end{scope}
\end{tikzpicture}\end{center}
The curved surface has area $(2\pi r)h$. Give it a small radial thickness $\Delta r$ to approximate a shell volume:
\[\Delta V\approx(2\pi r)h\,\Delta r.\]
For a cylindrical shell with constant height $h$, the exact finite-thickness volume is
\begin{align*}
\Delta V&=\pi[(r+\Delta r)^2-r^2]h\\
 &=2\pi rh\,\Delta r+\pi h(\Delta r)^2.
\end{align*}
The second term vanishes in the integral limit. When the slice length varies, its variation is also handled by the limit. Thus $dV=2\pi rh\,dr$.
\topic{A comparison that prevents memorization}
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{p{1.15in}p{2.25in}p{2.6in}}\toprule
 & Washer & Shell\\\midrule
What is added? & Circular cross-sections & Thin cylindrical layers\\[5pt]
Slice direction & Perpendicular to axis & Parallel to axis\\[5pt]
Distances & Outer and inner radius & Radius and slice length\\[5pt]
Volume element & $\pi(R^2-r^2)\,dt$ & $2\pi rh\,dt$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
\pauseq{Does rotation about the $x$-axis always require $dx$?}
\textit{No.} Washers use $dx$ here, but horizontal shells use $dy$. The slice direction determines the differential.
\textbf{Boundary check.} If the top, bottom, left, or right boundary changes formula within the interval, split the integral at that transition or choose another slice direction. A single formula must describe every slice where it is used.

\topic{8. Practice solutions and geometric checks}
\textbf{Practice A: washers about the $x$-axis.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item A vertical slice extends from $y=x$ to $y=3$, for $0\leq x\leq3$.
\item Distances from $y=0$ give $R=3$ and $r=x$.
\item The washer area is $\pi(9-x^2)$, so
\[
V=\pi\int_0^3(9-x^2)\,dx
=\pi\left[9x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^3
=\pi(27-9)=\boxed{18\pi}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\intuition{The solid is a cylinder of radius $3$ and length $3$ with a cone removed. The cylinder volume is $27\pi$ and the cone volume is $\tfrac13\pi(3^2)(3)=9\pi$. Their difference is $18\pi$.}
\textbf{Practice B: shells about the $y$-axis.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Use vertical slices, parallel to the vertical rotation axis.
\item The radius is the distance from $x=0$ to the slice: $r=x$.
\item The height is top minus bottom: $h=(4-x)-0=4-x$.
\item Integrate over $0\leq x\leq4$:
\begin{align*}
V&=2\pi\int_0^4x(4-x)\,dx
=2\pi\int_0^4(4x-x^2)\,dx\\
 &=2\pi\left[2x^2-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^4
 =2\pi\left(32-\frac{64}{3}\right)=\boxed{\frac{64\pi}{3}}.
\end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
\intuition{The solid is a cone with radius $4$ and height $4$. Its volume $\tfrac13\pi(4^2)(4)=64\pi/3$ independently confirms the shell calculation.}
\topic{If you get stuck}
Ask: Where is the axis? Which direction is the slice? What shape does it sweep out? Where do the radius and slice length begin and end?
\textbf{Exit-check answer:} a vertical slice is parallel to the $y$-axis and sweeps out a cylindrical layer. A horizontal slice is perpendicular and sweeps out a circular cross-section, with or without a hole.
