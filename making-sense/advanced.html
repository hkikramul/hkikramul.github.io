SEMINAR NOTES / IDEAS FOR BIOMEDICAL RESEARCH

Two ways to uncover patterns hidden in complex data

Brief learning notes inspired by Dr. Ker-Chau Li’s UNLV seminar and ASA Nevada Chapter talk. These methods inform my developing research interests.

LIQUID ASSOCIATION · CHANGING RELATIONSHIPS

When does the relationship between two genes change?

Research idea. Two genes may move together in one biological state and in opposite directions in another. Their overall correlation can hide this pattern. Liquid association uses a third variable to investigate the changing relationship.

Novel contribution. A statistical score makes it feasible to screen many gene triplets for changing coexpression, extending analysis beyond one fixed pairwise correlation.

Biomedical application. The original study examined yeast gene regulation. A later cancer study used liquid association to examine gene expression, cell invasion, and anticancer compound sensitivity—helping generate hypotheses about context-dependent drug response.

Original paper · Li (2002), PNAS ↗

Cancer application · Hsu et al. (2013), BMC Medicine ↗

Association supports discovery; it does not establish a causal mechanism or a treatment recommendation.
SLICED INVERSE REGRESSION · DIMENSION REDUCTION

Which combinations of measurements matter for an outcome?

Research idea. Group observations into slices of the outcome, compare their average predictor profiles, and find a few informative directions. SIR studies predictors given the outcome to simplify modeling the outcome given predictors.

Novel contribution. SIR introduced an inverse-regression approach to finding dimension-reduction directions without first fitting a full outcome model. Unlike PCA, it uses the outcome to guide reduction.

Biomedical relevance. For many biomarker measurements, SIR can help identify combinations associated with a measured health outcome, then support simpler exploratory models. This is an illustrative use case; the 1991 paper is a methodological foundation, not a clinical validation study.

Original paper · Li (1991), JASA ↗

Read the paper · UCLA repository PDF ↗

Recovery depends on assumptions; symmetric relationships can be missed. High-dimensional and censored survival data need suitable adaptations and validation.
