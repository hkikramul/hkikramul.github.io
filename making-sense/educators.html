Statistics teaching: research into practice

A focused reading shelf for my development as a statistics educator. Start with the ASA guidance and a practical activity book, then connect each reading to one discussion activity and one assessment question. These are selected resources, not a universal ranking.

START HERE · PROFESSIONAL GUIDANCE

ASA GAISE College Report

American Statistical Association · living report

Guidance for introductory statistics and data science, including learning outcomes, assessment examples, and a section on AI. Use it to audit a lesson’s goals, activity, and assessment.

Read the current guidance ↗

Free online · Consult the living report for updates.

ARTICLE · INFERENCE

The Introductory Statistics Course: A Ptolemaic Curriculum?

George W. Cobb · 2007

A foundational argument for rethinking introductory inference. Teaching task: let students explore a randomization distribution before introducing a formal test.

Read the article ↗

Open access · Foundational reading.

ARTICLE · COMPUTING & CURRICULUM

Data Science in Statistics Curricula: Preparing Students to “Think with Data”

Hardin and colleagues · 2015

Examples of integrating data science into statistics curricula. Teaching task: build a small exercise that joins data cleaning, visualization, interpretation, and reproducible code.

Read the author manuscript ↗

Free author manuscript · Published in The American Statistician.

ARTICLE · VISUAL COMMUNICATION

Teaching and Learning Data Visualization: Ideas and Assignments

Deborah Nolan & Jamis Perrett · 2016

Ideas for making graphics part of statistical education. Teaching task: ask students to revise a misleading plot and explain how the revised design supports the statistical question.

Read the author manuscript ↗

Free author manuscript · Published in The American Statistician.

BOOK · PRACTICAL TEACHING

Teaching Statistics: A Bag of Tricks

Andrew Gelman & Deborah Nolan · 2nd edition, 2017

My first choice for practical teaching preparation: classroom examples and activities, including topics for more advanced courses. Adapt one activity to a specific learning goal rather than adding it only for entertainment.

View the publisher’s page ↗

Publisher preview · Check institutional access or borrowing.

BOOK · PEDAGOGY & LEARNING RESEARCH

Developing Students’ Statistical Reasoning: Connecting Research and Teaching Practice

Garfield, Ben-Zvi, Chance, Medina, Roseth & Zieffler · 2008

A deeper research foundation for teaching variability, distributions, inference, and modeling. Start with the chapters on learning environments and assessment, then select a concept you currently teach.

Explore the book and chapters ↗

Publisher preview · Check institutional access or borrowing.

OPEN TEXTBOOK · MODERN INTRODUCTORY STATISTICS

Introduction to Modern Statistics

Mine Çetinkaya-Rundel & Johanna Hardin · 2nd edition

A course-design model for exploratory analysis, multivariable thinking, simulation, and traditional inference. Use the R tutorials and labs as inspiration for scaffolded discussions.

Read the book and teaching resources ↗

Free web text and PDF · Some instructor resources require verification.

OPEN TEXTBOOK · COMPUTATIONAL INFERENCE

Computational and Inferential Thinking: The Foundations of Data Science

Ani Adhikari, John DeNero & David Wagner

A complementary Python-based model for teaching computation alongside inference. Explore an example of simulation or resampling and adapt the statistical idea to your students’ computing background.

Read the online textbook ↗

Free online · Python-based.

ONGOING DEVELOPMENT · STATISTICS EDUCATION

CAUSEweb: resources, research, and professional development

Consortium for the Advancement of Undergraduate Statistics Education

A continuing source of teaching activities and professional learning. Use the core readings index to follow a topic in depth and evaluate activities against your own course goals.

Explore CAUSEweb ↗

Free resource hub · Includes simulation-based inference and teaching networks.

A sustainable cycle: define one learning goal → choose a task → check students’ reasoning → revise. Keep reusable lesson notes, record the source and license of borrowed materials, and review this reading list each academic year.
