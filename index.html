JSM 2027 · LITERATURE REVIEW

Regression reasoning
in biomedical AI research.

Task and rubric audit · Updated October 14, 2026

This review asks whether current AI benchmarks test regression-specific judgment: model specification, recognition of model limitations, diagnostics and remedies, and reliable uncertainty—not merely whether a regression model appears in a task.

Preliminary novelty finding. The six sources below evaluate biomedical hypothesis validation, causal analysis, clinical research workflows, or predictive performance. MedResearchBench explicitly scores model specification and includes examples such as survey-weighted logistic regression, splines, piecewise regression, and Cox regression. Across the task materials reviewed, however, I found no benchmark that systematically combines controlled regression misspecification, diagnostic/remedy scoring, and repeated-simulation assessment of inferential bias and confidence-interval coverage. This is a candidate gap from a targeted audit, not a claim that no such benchmark exists; a broader systematic search and independent review are still needed.

Review status: task materials and scoring rubrics inspected for BioDSA-1K, CauSciBench, MedResearchBench, and the reproductive-health prediction study. CausalReasoningBenchmark and CausalDS are included as related reading; their complete task-level materials still need review. JSM 2027 is a target for this developing project.

2026 · medRxiv preprint

Its 16 clinical tasks use six scoring dimensions, including statistical methodology, results accuracy, and confounding sensitivity. The reviewed checklists include survey-weighted logistic regression, nonlinear splines, piecewise regression, Cox models, and interaction tests.

Regression-specific finding: this is the closest overlap. Reuben Sarwal et al. · 2026 · Cell Reports Medicine

Tests code generation for four DREAM challenge prediction tasks, including continuous gestational-age regression. Scoring covers data preparation, model fitting, test-set performance, and plots.

Regression-specific finding: it evaluates predictive performance (including RMSE), not inferential regression diagnostics, coefficient uncertainty, or coverage under known model misspecification.

Published article ↗ · Full text ↗

Working project frame: A simulation-based evaluation of AI agents’ regression model specification, diagnostics, and inferential calibration for biomedical outcomes. A feasible first pilot would focus on one family (for example, logistic regression), prespecified failure conditions, accepted alternative analyses, and separate scores for reasoning, computation, estimation, and uncertainty.

Next steps: complete the task-level audit of the two related causal benchmarks; broaden the search to statistical/data-science agent benchmarks; have biostatisticians review the proposed scenarios and scoring; then run a small pilot before making a “first” or field-wide novelty claim.

TEACHING PHILOSOPHY

From a meaningful question to a defensible conclusion.

My goal is to help students become thoughtful users of data: able to ask a clear question, examine how evidence was collected, recognize variability, and explain what an analysis can and cannot establish. I connect intuitive explanations and visual representations with mathematical reasoning, so that procedures have a purpose and conclusions remain grounded in context.

I aim to organize statistics discussions around investigation: question, data, exploration, inference, and communication. Real examples from health and everyday life can make decisions about sampling, measurement, confounding, and missing data visible. Simulation offers a bridge from informal ideas about chance to sampling distributions, randomization tests, and confidence intervals; formulas then deepen that understanding.

A supportive classroom also needs intellectual challenge. I encourage questions and treat mistakes as opportunities to diagnose reasoning. My teaching development priorities include structured peer discussion, brief checks of understanding, scaffolded computing, and clear feedback. I seek to make participation accessible through readable materials, multiple representations, and opportunities to think before speaking.

Assessment should reveal understanding as well as procedural accuracy. I aim to align tasks with explicit learning goals and ask students to justify a method, interpret uncertainty, and critique a conclusion. For example, a correct calculation should be accompanied by an explanation of the population, assumptions, and limits of the inference. Short written explanations and transfer problems can expose misconceptions that a numerical answer alone misses.

Responsible analysis is part of statistical literacy. My teaching goals include reproducible workflows, transparent data provenance, privacy, and a careful distinction between association and causation. Where course policy permits AI tools, students should disclose their use, verify outputs, and explain the reasoning themselves. Technology should support learning without replacing judgment.

I view teaching as an ongoing process of inquiry. I plan to use student questions, formative assessments, peer feedback, and reflective notes to refine discussion activities with the course instructor. These commitments draw on ASA GAISE guidance and statistics education research; the approaches below describe my continuing development rather than a claim that every method has already been implemented.

SEMINAR NOTES / IDEAS FOR BIOMEDICAL RESEARCH

Two ways to uncover patterns hidden in complex data

Brief learning notes inspired by Dr. Ker-Chau Li’s UNLV seminar and ASA Nevada Chapter talk. These methods inform my developing research interests.

LIQUID ASSOCIATION · CHANGING RELATIONSHIPS

When does the relationship between two genes change?

Research idea. Two genes may move together in one biological state and in opposite directions in another. Their overall correlation can hide this pattern. Liquid association uses a third variable to investigate the changing relationship.

Novel contribution. A statistical score makes it feasible to screen many gene triplets for changing coexpression, extending analysis beyond one fixed pairwise correlation.

Biomedical application. The original study examined yeast gene regulation. A later cancer study used liquid association to examine gene expression, cell invasion, and anticancer compound sensitivity—helping generate hypotheses about context-dependent drug response.

Original paper · Li (2002), PNAS ↗

Cancer application · Hsu et al. (2013), BMC Medicine ↗

Association supports discovery; it does not establish a causal mechanism or a treatment recommendation.
SLICED INVERSE REGRESSION · DIMENSION REDUCTION

Which combinations of measurements matter for an outcome?

Research idea. Group observations into slices of the outcome, compare their average predictor profiles, and find a few informative directions. SIR studies predictors given the outcome to simplify modeling the outcome given predictors.

Novel contribution. SIR introduced an inverse-regression approach to finding dimension-reduction directions without first fitting a full outcome model. Unlike PCA, it uses the outcome to guide reduction.

Biomedical relevance. For many biomarker measurements, SIR can help identify combinations associated with a measured health outcome, then support simpler exploratory models. This is an illustrative use case; the 1991 paper is a methodological foundation, not a clinical validation study.

Original paper · Li (1991), JASA ↗

Read the paper · UCLA repository PDF ↗

Recovery depends on assumptions; symmetric relationships can be missed. High-dimensional and censored survival data need suitable adaptations and validation.
